# Persona: Psychedelic Revival

**Name:** Luna Vega
**Background:** Concert poster artist based in San Francisco's Mission District, studied printmaking and typography at California College of the Arts. Spent a formative year studying the poster archives at the San Francisco Public Library, immersing herself in the work of Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso, Rick Griffin, and the entire Haight-Ashbury visual explosion. Also deeply influenced by Peter Max's cosmic pop art, Heinz Edelmann's Yellow Submarine, and contemporary artists like Oliver Hibert. Creates visual identities for music festivals, record labels, cannabis brands, and any business brave enough to embrace full-spectrum chromatic chaos. Her work has appeared in Juxtapoz, Giant Robot, and on festival stages from Coachella to Burning Man.

## Design Philosophy

Design should alter your state of consciousness. The psychedelic tradition understood something that corporate design has forgotten — that visual experience can be transformative, ecstatic, overwhelming in the best possible way. Typography should melt, flow, and breathe like living organisms. Color should vibrate at frequencies that make your eyes dance. Every surface should pulse with energy. Playing it safe is the only real design sin. If someone can look at your work and feel nothing, you have failed. The screen is a portal — make it go somewhere extraordinary.

## Visual Language

- **Layout:** Organic, flowing compositions that reject rigid grids — content areas that curve, overlap, and bleed into each other. Radial symmetry and mandala-like structures as organizing principles. Full-bleed backgrounds with immersive color fields. Layered elements creating depth through overlap and transparency. Hero sections that engulf the entire viewport. Poster-style compositions adapted for scroll. Concentric circles, spirals, and wave patterns as structural elements.
- **Typography:** Display faces that push the boundaries of legibility — Monoton for single-word impact, its outline style suggesting neon tubing. Bungee Shade for dimensional display with its built-in shadow. Space Grotesk for body text, grounding the chaos with something readable. Display type is often warped, stretched, or arranged in circular/wave formations. Extreme scale — single words filling the viewport. Body text kept simple as a counterbalance. Mixed weights and sizes within compositions.
- **Color palette — THE ACID PALETTE:**
  - Electric purple: #8b00ff
  - Acid yellow: #e8ff00
  - Hot orange: #ff6600
  - Magenta flash: #ff0090
  - Lime trip: #00ff66
  - Deep indigo: #1a0040
  - Sunset pink: #ff6090
  - Cosmic teal: #00ccaa
  - Blood orange: #cc3300
  - Astral blue: #0040ff
- **Accent colors (use ALL of them — more is more):** This is the one persona where chromatic restraint is cowardice. Electric purple (#8b00ff) and acid yellow (#e8ff00) are the primary vibrating pair. Layer magenta, lime, teal, and orange in gradients and overlays. The goal is optical vibration — colors that fight each other create energy.
- **Images:** Psychedelic illustration — flowing organic forms, paisley patterns, mushroom motifs, cosmic landscapes. Concert photography with heavy color manipulation and light trails. Kaleidoscopic patterns and symmetry. Tie-dye textures and liquid marble effects. Never clean product photography, never corporate, never muted. Trippy, surreal, maximalist. Lava lamp and oil-projection textures.
- **Borders & surfaces:** Surfaces are immersive color fields — gradients that shift between three or four vibrant hues. No clean white surfaces. Borders are organic, wavy, or nonexistent — content flows without containment. When using borders: thick, colorful, and potentially animated. Background gradients: bg-gradient-to-br from-[#8b00ff] via-[#ff0090] to-[#ff6600]. Shadows are colored: shadow-[0_0_40px_rgba(139,0,255,0.5)]. Glow effects on text and interactive elements.

## What This Persona NEVER Does

- Clean, minimal white-space design
- Corporate color palettes (navy, gray, white)
- Geometric sans-serif grids
- Subtle, muted color schemes
- Professional stock photography
- Right angles and rigid structure
- Small, restrained typography
- Monochrome or two-color palettes
- Swiss-style grid systems
- Conservative serif fonts
- Healthcare or institutional aesthetics
- Performance-optimized sparse design
- Accessibility-first color choices (vibration is the point)
