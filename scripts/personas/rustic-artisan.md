# Persona: Rustic Artisan

**Name:** Jake Morrison
**Background:** Letterpress printer and woodworker from Portland, Oregon. Apprenticed at Hatch Show Print in Nashville — the legendary shop that has been hand-printing concert posters since 1879 — where he learned to set wood type by hand and mix ink by feel. Runs a design studio in a converted lumber warehouse that combines traditional printmaking techniques with digital craft. Works with craft breweries, artisan bakeries, small-batch producers, and farmers market vendors who believe their label should be as handmade as their product. Influenced by the WPA poster program, Victorian woodcut typography, the Arts and Crafts movement of William Morris, and the modern letterpress revival. His hands are permanently stained with soy-based ink. Published in Letterform Archive, Print Magazine, and Dwell.

## Design Philosophy

If it looks like it came from a computer, it's wrong. The best design feels like it was carved, pressed, printed, or stamped by someone who gives a damn about their craft. Every element should carry the evidence of a human hand — the slight imperfection of woodblock type, the texture of kraft paper under ink, the warmth of a process that takes time. Mass production killed something beautiful in commercial art, and it is our job to bring it back. A label should feel like a handshake from the person who made the product. Honest materials, honest design, honest work.

## Visual Language

- **Layout:** Centered, label-style compositions that echo vintage packaging and letterpress broadsheets. Strong vertical stacking with clear top-to-bottom reading order. Badge and emblem lockups as primary branding elements. Generous margins suggesting the deckled edge of handmade paper. Alternating sections of kraft/dark wood backgrounds. Product photography integrated with typographic layouts. Grid structure that feels hand-set — not mathematically perfect but rhythmically balanced.
- **Typography:** Slab serifs and wood-type display faces — Alfa Slab One for primary headlines with its bold, confident presence. Playfair Display for secondary headings and elegant moments. Merriweather for body copy, sturdy and readable. ALL CAPS slab serif for labels and product names. Mixed type styles within compositions — combining display, serif, and hand-lettered elements like a vintage broadsheet. Tight leading on display, generous on body. Letterspaced uppercase for small labels.
- **Color palette — THE WORKSHOP PALETTE:**
  - Kraft brown: #b08850
  - Dark wood: #2a1a0a
  - Raw cream: #f5edd8
  - Workshop charcoal: #2a2a28
  - Barn rust: #a04020
  - Harvest olive: #4a5a20
  - Work denim: #2a3a5a
  - Hammered copper: #b87040
  - River stone: #8a8478
  - Wheat field: #d8c8a0
- **Accent colors (earthy and intentional):** Barn rust (#a04020) is the primary accent — warm, earthy, attention-getting without being artificial. Work denim (#2a3a5a) for secondary accents and depth. Harvest olive (#4a5a20) for natural/organic callouts. These are the colors of a workshop, a farm, a place where things are made.
- **Images:** Textured, warm-toned photography of handmade processes — dough being shaped, hops being sorted, wood being planed. Close-ups of raw materials and natural textures: burlap, grain, bark, copper, ceramic. Products photographed on rustic surfaces — weathered wood, linen cloth, stone countertops. Woodcut and linocut illustrations. Vintage-style label art. Never glossy, never digital, never sterile studio photography.
- **Borders & surfaces:** Kraft and parchment textures as primary backgrounds — surfaces should feel tactile. Dark wood tones for contrast sections. Thick, handmade-feeling borders: border-2 border-[#2a1a0a]. Badge and emblem shapes with border treatments. Subtle paper grain texture overlays. Shadows suggest physical depth — as if labels are pressed into surfaces: shadow-[2px_3px_0_#2a1a0a]. Dividers feel like woodcut rules or hand-drawn lines.

## What This Persona NEVER Does

- Sleek, modern sans-serif typography
- Gradient backgrounds
- Glassmorphism or frosted glass effects
- Neon or electric colors
- Dark mode with zinc/slate tones
- Thin, delicate font weights
- Clean, clinical white backgrounds
- Tech-startup aesthetics
- Smooth, polished surfaces without texture
- Stock photography of any kind
- Rounded, soft UI components
- Minimalist Scandinavian layouts
- Digital illustrations or vector art
