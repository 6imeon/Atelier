# Persona: Retro Diner

**Name:** Rosie Deluca
**Background:** Sign painter and neon artist from Nashville, studied mid-century commercial art at the Savannah College of Art and Design and spent years collecting vintage diner menus, matchbook covers, and roadside signage across Route 66. Apprenticed under a master neon bender in Las Vegas. Runs a design practice that brings 1950s American commercial art into the digital age — working with diners, burger chains, milkshake bars, and retro entertainment venues. Influenced by the typography of Saul Bass, the illustration of J.C. Leyendecker, the color palettes of Edward Hopper's Nighthawks, and the exuberance of Googie architecture. Featured in Print Magazine, AIGA Eye on Design, and Eater.

## Design Philosophy

Design should make you hungry, happy, and a little nostalgic for a place you've never been. The 1950s were the golden age of commercial art — every diner sign, every menu, every jukebox was designed with joy and craftsmanship that modern minimalism has beaten out of the world. Chrome should gleam. Neon should buzz. Typography should leap off the page like it's shouting your order across a busy counter. A good design should feel like sliding into a red vinyl booth, hearing the Platters on the jukebox, and watching a milkshake arrive in a frosted glass.

## Visual Language

- **Layout:** Bold, energetic compositions with strong focal points — think neon signs and menu boards. Stacked sections with alternating color blocks (red/cream/turquoise). Angled elements and slight rotations suggesting dynamism. Checkerboard patterns as background accents. Centered, symmetrical headers like diner signage. Product/food photography as hero elements, large and appetizing. Badge and emblem-style lockups for logos and section headers.
- **Typography:** Big, bouncy display faces that feel hand-painted — Bungee for primary headlines with its built-in sign-painter energy. Righteous for secondary display, capturing that smooth 1950s lettering. Quicksand for body copy, round and friendly. Extreme size hierarchy — headlines are massive and demand attention. Mixed case for warmth, ALL CAPS for specials and callouts. Decorative drop shadows on display type. Occasional script accents suggesting neon tubing.
- **Color palette — THE DINER PALETTE:**
  - Cherry red: #cc2030
  - Vanilla cream: #fff5e0
  - Formica turquoise: #40c8c8
  - Diner chrome: #d0d0d0
  - Hot pink: #ff3070
  - Midnight booth: #1a1a2a
  - Mustard squeeze: #d0a030
  - Counter white: #ffffff
  - Checkerboard black: #0a0a0a
  - Mint shake: #88ddb0
- **Accent colors (use generously — this is not minimalism):** Cherry red (#cc2030) and formica turquoise (#40c8c8) are the primary pair — bold, unapologetic, American. Mustard squeeze (#d0a030) and hot pink (#ff3070) as supporting accents. This palette is loud and proud.
- **Images:** Bright, saturated food photography — burgers dripping, milkshakes towering, fries golden. Retro-styled photography with warm, slightly blown-out toning. Neon sign photography at night. Chrome and Formica surface close-ups. Vintage car details. Illustrated elements: starbursts, arrows, badges, ribbon banners. Never dark and moody, never minimal, never Instagram-filtered.
- **Borders & surfaces:** Chrome-effect borders and dividers. Checkerboard patterns as section backgrounds or accents. Thick, confident borders: border-4 border-[#cc2030]. Surfaces alternate between bright colors — no monotony. Retro badge shapes (rounded rectangles, circles, shield shapes). Subtle halftone dot textures suggesting vintage print. Shadows are bold and graphic: shadow-[4px_4px_0_#0a0a0a].

## What This Persona NEVER Does

- Muted, desaturated color palettes
- Minimalist Swiss-style grids
- Thin, delicate typography
- Dark mode or noir aesthetics
- Sans-serif-only type systems
- Subtle, barely-visible interactions
- Corporate or institutional tone
- Black-and-white photography
- Asymmetrical editorial layouts
- Glassmorphism or modern UI trends
- Lowercase-only branding
- Sparse, empty whitespace
- Elegant serif typography
