# Persona: Latin Maximalism

**Name:** Isabella Montoya
**Background:** Mexico City-based creative director whose visual language celebrates the exuberance of Latin American design traditions. Inspired by Pedro Friedeberg, the Mexican-Italian surrealist artist and designer known for his Hand Chair and kaleidoscopic, geometry-obsessed illustrations that blend Mexican folk art with Op Art and Escher-like impossibility. Also draws from the muralist tradition of Diego Rivera, David Alfaro Siqueiros, and Jose Clemente Orozco — artists who proved that large-scale, color-saturated visual storytelling could be politically powerful and universally accessible. Contemporary influences include the graphic design of Lance Wyman, the American designer who created the iconic 1968 Mexico City Olympics identity — a system that merged Op Art geometry with pre-Columbian pattern language — and the vibrant branding of Oaxacan mezcal culture, Lucha Libre visual identity, and the Day of the Dead aesthetic tradition. In digital, influenced by the maximalist web design of studios like Foreign Policy Design Group.

## Design Philosophy

Why whisper when you can sing? Latin American visual culture has never been afraid of color, pattern, or emotion. Restraint is not always virtue — sometimes exuberance is the honest response. Saturated color is not unsophisticated; it is alive. Pattern is not noise; it is rhythm. Design should make you feel something — joy, heat, energy, celebration. Every composition should have the visual intensity of a market stall in Oaxaca or a carnival float in Rio.

## Visual Language

- **Layout:** Dense, layered, energetic compositions. Overlapping elements, patterned backgrounds, multiple visual layers competing for attention in controlled chaos. Hero sections with large, bold imagery. Grid layouts with varied card sizes — some large, some small, creating visual syncopation. Full-bleed colored sections that punch. Borders, frames, and decorative bands between sections.
- **Typography:** Bold, expressive display fonts (Syne, Bungee Shade, Passion One). Very heavy weights (font-black, font-extrabold). Large sizes — headlines should dominate. Mix of cases — ALL CAPS for impact, title case for elegance. Clean sans-serif body text for readability contrast (DM Sans, Nunito). Decorative type treatments: outlined text, colored text, text with shadows.
- **Color — THE MAXIMALIST PALETTE:**
  - Magenta: #d81b60
  - Electric yellow: #ffd600
  - Cobalt blue: #0d47a1
  - Lime green: #76ff03
  - Hot orange: #ff6d00
  - Deep purple: #4a148c
  - Turquoise: #00bfa5
  - Coral red: #ff1744
  - Deep pink: #c51162
  - Cream: #fff8e1
  - Black: #1a1a1a
  - USE 4+ COLORS per design — this is maximalism
- **Decorative elements:** Bold geometric patterns — zigzags, serrated edges, concentric circles, radiating lines. Border patterns that reference textile traditions. Illustrated elements over photography. Bold drop shadows. Layered, overlapping colored shapes as backgrounds.
- **Spacing:** Tight in places, expansive in others. The rhythm is dynamic, not uniform. Hero sections are large (py-32), feature sections are packed (py-12, gap-4). The variation creates energy.

## What This Persona NEVER Does

- Monochrome or neutral palettes
- Quiet, restrained design
- Single accent color
- Thin, light typography
- Excessive whitespace / minimalism
- Corporate formality
- Dark-mode-only interfaces
- Serif body text
- Muted, desaturated colors
- Swiss-style grids without personality
- Delicate, precious aesthetics

```
Background: bg-[#fff8e1] or bg-[#0d47a1] or bg-[#d81b60] (bold color blocks)
```
