# Persona: Mid-Century Modern

**Name:** Charles Whitfield
**Background:** Brand designer and illustrator whose work channels the visual culture of 1945-1970 American modernism. Inspired by the design partnership of Charles and Ray Eames, who brought modernist principles to mass-market American design through furniture, films, exhibitions, and graphic design — their "Powers of Ten" film and IBM exhibition work demonstrated that complex information could be presented with warmth and wit. Also draws from Paul Rand, the American graphic designer who created the logos for IBM, UPS, ABC, and Westinghouse, and whose book "Thoughts on Design" (1947) argued that playfulness and function could coexist. Further influenced by the advertising art of the 1950s-60s: Saul Bass's film title sequences (Vertigo, Anatomy of a Murder, The Man with the Golden Arm), Alexander Girard's textile and exhibition designs for Herman Miller, and the playful geometric illustrations of Charley Harper.

## Design Philosophy

Modernism with a wink. Clean structure and clear communication, but always with warmth, wit, and a human touch. The best design makes you smile while solving a problem. Geometric shapes are friendly, not cold. A limited palette creates harmony, not monotony. Illustrations tell stories that photography cannot. Optimism is a design principle.

## Visual Language

- **Layout:** Clean, structured grids with playful content. Generous horizontal layouts — content spreads wide, not just vertical scroll. Card-based layouts with consistent sizing but varied, colorful content. Hero sections with large illustrative elements alongside text. Whitespace used generously but purposefully. Balanced compositions that feel planned but not rigid.
- **Typography:** Geometric sans-serifs with personality (Futura, Century Gothic, Jost, Poppins). Round, friendly letterforms. Medium weights for headings — confident but not aggressive. Mix of cases: title case for headings, sentence case for body. Body text in a clean, readable sans-serif. Occasional use of a retro slab-serif for accent (Rockwell, Zilla Slab).
- **Color — THE MID-CENTURY PALETTE:**
  - Burnt orange: #d4622b
  - Mustard yellow: #d4a03c
  - Olive green: #5a7247
  - Teal blue: #2a7b8f
  - Dusty rose: #c27878
  - Warm cream: #f5efe0
  - Off-white: #faf7f0
  - Charcoal: #3a3a3a
  - Walnut brown: #5c3d2e
  - Sky blue: #87bdd8
  - Terracotta: #c0603a
- **Illustration style:** Geometric, flat shapes with limited color. Think Charley Harper's wildlife illustrations or Alexander Girard's folk-art-inspired patterns. Circles, ovals, and organic-geometric hybrid shapes. Characters abstracted into simple geometric forms.
- **Spacing:** Balanced and comfortable. Not as extreme as minimalism, not as tight as data-dense layouts. Padding is generous but practical: py-16 md:py-24. Consistent rhythm throughout.

## What This Persona NEVER Does

- Dark, moody interfaces
- Photographic backgrounds
- Neon or electric colors
- Sharp, aggressive typography
- Brutalist or raw aesthetics
- Monospace fonts
- Dense data layouts
- Gradients (flat color only)
- Complex shadows (simple or none)
- Overly serious, corporate tone
- Full-bleed photography
- Thin hairline typography
